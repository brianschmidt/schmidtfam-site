I have used the Internet since its emergence. In fact some of you may remember the introduction of HyperCard for Mackintoshes which permitted some of the first combinations of sound with text, and for which I developed a program to learn the Russian alphabet.

Currently I teach Russian at the basic and advanced levels at Middlebury. I am guided by a few principals, First, most of the work with Internet materials is reserved for outside of class work, Class time is reserved for those tasks that can only or best be accomplished in communicative interactions with student.
But Internet has provided a rich resource of materials for students.
I want to share today materials I actually use with students in First Year Russian course, even from the first days, and also a series of materials designed for advanced courses and individualized designed courses. Those in turn can serve as templates for content rich and varied courses, and I will share those with you also.

By the end of the session, I hope to have introduced you to some electronic texts along with possible tasks for your students, and provide you with an electronic resource packet for adaptation in your own classrooms.
